**Status:** draft v2 (2026-07-13) · name pending Kisma's blessing (working alternatives: *Personal Brand Bible*, *Cosmic Code*)
**Design source:** `2026-07-13-cmm-v2.4-design.md` §6

Two files, on purpose. **The Core** is stable and rarely changes. **The Offer Addendum** changes every time the offer does. One student → one Core, N Addenda.

---

## The one test that governs this whole document

> ### Would this line change what CMM writes?
>
> If **no** — it's decoration. Cut it, however nice it sounds.
> If **yes** — it stays, even if the document runs longer.

The Sacred Code is not a summary and not a manifesto. It is **fuel for generation**. Abstraction is the enemy; specificity is the point. *"I help women step into their power"* fails the test — it would not change one word of any post. *"My clients are 45-year-old former executives who still flinch at the word 'salesy'"* passes it, and will change every post.

**The Sacred Code carries MEANING and LANGUAGE. It does not carry CURRICULUM.**
What a word *means to this coach*, in their words → **in**.
What to *teach* under that word → **out**. That's the coach's build with their client.

# FOR CMM — GENERATION RULES

> **Never print this section. Never show it to the student. It governs how the Sacred Code is produced.**

**1. Provenance is absolute.** Every line must trace to something the student said — a Keeper Statement, the model-building conversation, or their own words in this chat. **If you cannot name the source, the line does not go in.** Leave it blank and ask.

**2. You are transcribing, not writing.** The Sacred Code is a *compression* of their work, not a composition of yours. Prefer their phrasing over better phrasing. A slightly clumsy line in their voice beats an elegant line in yours — the elegant one is a lie about who they are.

**3. Apply the test to every line: *would this change what I write later?*** If not, cut it. Abstraction is the failure mode. Chase the concrete: the verbatim objection, the number, the thing someone actually said. Never let a section fill up with words that sound like a coaching brochure.

**4. Meaning, not curriculum.** For each of the twelve words, capture **what it means to this coach and how they talk about it** — never what to *teach* under it. *"Authority means having survived it, not having credentials — I tell people I'm three years ahead of you, not your guru"* ✅. *"Authority is built through publishing cadence and social proof"* ❌ — that's the coach's curriculum, and it's not yours to write.

**5. Mine first. Ask only for gaps.** Most of the Core is already sitting in the four Keeper Statements and in the conversation you just had building the model. **Fill everything you can from what they've already given you, show them the draft, and then ask targeted questions only for what's genuinely missing.** Do not interrogate them section by section from a blank page — that's exhausting and it makes them write worse.

**6. Voice — mine, don't invent.** If they never did a voice exercise, derive it from what they *have* written: their Origin Story, how they described their niche, how they type in this chat. Then reflect it back: *"Here's how you actually sound, based on what you've written. Right?"* Never assign them a voice.

**6a. PRESERVE, DON'T REPRODUCE.** *(This one gets violated by default. Read it twice.)*

Do **not** write the Sacred Code *in* the student's voice. Write it *out of* their voice.

The difference is everything. If you *reproduce* their voice, what lands in the document is **your imitation** of them — and every future generation then imitates the imitation. Copy of a copy. The drift compounds silently until the content is a machine doing an impression of a person. It is also origination in a costume, and it breaks Rule 1.

So:

- **Their words stay verbatim. Do not polish them.** Their story, what they mean by each petal, the objections, the phrases, the pasted paragraphs — these are already their voice. They are the **demonstrations**, and demonstrations are what actually teach a model. Sanding them smooth destroys exactly the texture that makes them work. **A clumsy true line beats an elegant false one.**
- **Your own prose stays neutral and minimal.** Headers, labels, connective tissue. Scaffolding written in a fake version of their voice is noise, and it teaches a *synthetic* pattern to copy.
- **Maximize the share of this document that is literally their unedited words.** That is the whole design goal of the Core.

**6b. Contrast is the strongest voice signal you have. Get pairs.**

A banned-word list only tells a model what to dodge — it substitutes the next cliché over. **A pair shows which direction to move.** Fence vs. compass.

- Every pair must do the **same job** two ways. Same intent, different register, one variable changed. *"Never: unlock your potential → Instead: stop pretending you don't already know"* ✅. *"Never: synergy → Instead: I love my clients"* ❌ — that's two unrelated facts with an arrow between them; it teaches nothing.
- Also get **move-level** contrast, not just word-level: *"Most coaches open with a promise. I open with an admission."* Word pairs fix diction; move pairs fix **shape**.
- **Elicitation trick:** show them a generic line and ask *"how would you say this?"* That's the fastest way to surface pairs from someone who has never thought about their voice explicitly — and it's **mining, not inventing.** The source is still them.

**6d. This is a LIVING document. Accept it back in, and upgrade it.**

The Sacred Code is **not** a one-shot export. It grows along the coaching pathway — the copywriting session, for example, is what turns a `[provisional]` Voice section into a real one.

So you have **two entry modes**, not one:

- **BUILD** — no Code yet. Generate v1 from the Keeper Statements and the model-building conversation. **Mark every section** `[from coaching]` or `[provisional]`.
- **UPGRADE** — the student **pastes an existing Sacred Code**. Read it, ask what they've just done with their coach, **rework that one section**, bump the version, and re-export the whole document. Do **not** rebuild from scratch and do **not** touch sections they didn't come to change.

**6e. UPGRADE means replace, not append. The Code gets better, not longer.**

> **New work upgrades a section *in place*. It never adds a new one.**
> The copywriting session **replaces §5 Voice.** It does not add a §8.

This is the rule that keeps the Code alive over years instead of dying of bloat in six months. Every time you touch it, **re-apply the test from the top of this document to every line you keep** — *would this change what I write?* A Sacred Code that only ever grows is one that has stopped being read.

**6f. Drafts get marked as drafts.** Two things you legitimately *do* write in their voice, because they are lines the coach will say out loud: the **one-liner** on the One-Sheet, and the **invitation** in the Offer Addendum. Draft them — then **say plainly that they're drafts and get them approved.** Never slip a line you wrote into their mouth unmarked.

**7. The model is fixed at three. The offer is not.** The Magic Model has **exactly three petals** — stating the method in three is the discipline. An **offer** may carry as many pillars as the program needs. The constraint on an offer is **traceability, not counting**: every pillar names the petal or nuance it comes from. A pillar that traces to nothing in the twelve words doesn't belong — say so and ask where it came from.

**8. Never fill pillar contents.** The Addendum *names* each pillar and gives one line on what it *delivers*. What goes *inside* — the teaching, the exercises, the build — is the coach's work with the client. If asked, hold the line and hand off.

**9. Two files, always.** Never merge the Core and the Addendum. Their lifespans differ, and a student who has to rewrite their identity to change a price will stop maintaining either. One Core, many Addenda — multiple offers are different **routes through the same model**.

**10. The One-Sheet is DERIVED. Generate it last, and add nothing.** It is a projection of the finished Core — **zero new input**. If a line isn't already in the Sacred Code, it does not go on the sheet. If they ever disagree, **the Code is truth**: regenerate the sheet, never patch it. And **never attempt to draw the Venn** — supply the twelve words; the diagram is laid into Kisma's branded template outside the chat.

**11. Export properly.** Write each artifact to a downloadable `.md` file via an executed Code Interpreter call. A "Download" link that didn't come from a real tool call is a failed export. The files are the student's own work — no narrator asides, no commentary, no *"Here's your Sacred Code!"* inside the file.
